## 2026-06-04 — A GitHub issue's literal suggested fix would have introduced a BIGGER bug (#29)
**Finding:** Fixing #29 (let-out rental taxed gross — Sec 24(a) 30% deduction missing), the issue body's suggested fix was "apply 0.7 to `otherTaxable`." Tracing the data flow first showed that `otherTaxable` feeds BOTH `annualIncome.total` (the CASH basis → `annualSavings`) AND the tax `grossIncome`. Multiplying it by 0.7 would correctly cut tax but silently drop 30% of **real rent received** from savings — a *larger* error (₹72k/yr cash vs ₹24k/yr tax for the worked example), pushing FIRE later. Both the FinTech analyst and code review confirmed the divergence was the right call.
**Root cause / pattern:** The 30% standard deduction is a TAX fiction — the landlord receives full cash; only the taxable basis shrinks. The correct fix separates cash-vs-taxable: keep rental FULL in the cash total, subtract the 24a deduction from the tax `grossIncome` only (+ net the bridge rental cash at `gross·(1−mr·0.7)`). **An issue's suggested fix is a hypothesis, not gospel** — for financial math, ALWAYS trace where a value flows (cash basis vs tax basis vs cash-flow) before applying it.
**Gate-gap:** existing tests asserted FIRE-age sanity (shape) but nothing asserted let-out rental enters taxable income net of 30% — a shape-vs-substance miss. **Closed by** a *deterministic* differential test (`derive.spec.ts` #29: Rental-vs-Interest, asserts cash-total-equal + rental-taxed-less + saving bounded by the deduction) — a CI test, the good kind per the gate-gap retro below. Residuals (§24b interest, municipal taxes, bridge test coverage) tracked in #32.
